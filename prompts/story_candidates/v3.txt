STAGE: Invent exactly candidates_per_save complete, mutually distinct world concepts.
Use candidate IDs save_NN_candidate_1, save_NN_candidate_2, etc. for the supplied save.
Only the seed's role, broad setting family, emotion, general resource pressure, era,
and approximate severity are constraints. Time/weather constraints, when present,
can be developed to fit the world. They are not a bank of final details.

Invent together: title, specific setting, resource problem and food mechanics,
shelter, inhabitants, threat and danger type, central survival mechanic, rule,
consequence, escape, permanent cost and its type, anomaly, visual hook, emotional
mechanic, architecture, materials, time, weather, and palette. Explain palette_reason.
The resource problem may refine the broad resource pressure into something specific.
All candidates must offer different ways of living, not renamed variants.

Each world's main idea must fit in one short sentence a tired listener understands.
Build around one familiar setting and one simple strange condition or lasting cost.
Novelty should come from the place and that central idea, not extra rules or
elaborate equipment. Avoid concepts whose appeal or danger requires explaining a
machine, a warning gauge, several exceptions, a work rota or a multi-step procedure.
An impossible game-world rule is allowed when its effect is clear and consistent;
you do not need to rationalize it with engineering. Keep schema fields concise and
connected to the same simple idea instead of introducing a new mechanic per field.


Design the visual_hook as one immediately recognizable place or object with one
concrete unexplained detail, grounded in this world's anomaly or survival system.
It must remain readable as small cartridge-label art. The image should make viewers
wonder why that detail exists, without needing a caption to notice it. Generic fog,
an empty corridor, a glowing symbol or a mood adjective alone is not a visual hook.
Give each candidate a deliberate dominant hue, a contrasting accent and a clear
lighting identity in palette/palette_reason. Compare with selected sibling worlds
in the reel context: vary dominant hue, brightness, focal subject and composition,
not merely the names of similar muted colors. Avoid four smoky teal/gray/brown images.
Keep the palette credible for the place; do not assign color by survival tier.

Fill causal_logic with five concrete answers: danger_origin, rule_protection,
failure_consequence, cost_origin, reason_to_choose. Explanations must actually connect
facts, not merely assert that they are connected. Include a compact signature of the
setting, danger, rule, cost, anomaly and inhabitants. Each signature field is a short
literal concept phrase (roughly 3–8 words), faithful to the full concept. These are
comparison labels, not narration. Do not hide duplication behind new terminology.
No final narration, no final visual prompts. Later stages derive those from this concept.
If replacement_feedback is present, invent a substantially different concept, not a
cosmetic paraphrase of the rejected candidates.
