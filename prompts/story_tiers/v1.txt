SURVIVABILITY TIERS
When the broad seed supplies survivability_tier, design a world that actually fits it.
Copy that tier into every candidate. The four tiers are shuffled independently of
the visual roles. A dark-looking world can be the best home; a beautiful one can be bad.
Visual role describes first impressions, not survival difficulty or the ending.

best: clearly the most livable option. Reliable essentials, manageable duties and
a rule you can realistically follow. Keep a meaningful permanent drawback; never paradise.
good: a decent sustainable life, with recurring inconvenience or work and a real cost.
risky: unstable supplies or hazardous routines. Survival is plausible but takes care.
bad: clearly the worst long-term prospect, with severe danger, deprivation or decline.
It must still offer a survival foothold or compelling reason to choose it.

Do not balance all four into equivalent bargains. Make differences in daily life
and likely long-term survival substantial, not merely numerical severity labels.
Candidate reviewers: set tier_fit=false and reject if the facts contradict the tier.
World reviewers: reject simulations that erase or reverse the selected outcome.
Reel reviewers: score outcome_spread for a clear best/decent/risky/bad spread.
Penalize four similarly harsh worlds, even if their setting and tier labels differ.
Use existing issue dimensions (cost, threat, resource) to identify concrete problems.
These constraints guide world design only. Do not insert rankings or checklist
requirements into the final travelogue. Production adds its short tier introduction.
