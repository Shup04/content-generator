NARRATION ONLY — THREE SECOND-PERSON TRAVELOGUES FROM ONE APPROVED WORLD.

Write three distinct candidates, narration_1, narration_2, narration_3. Each is ONE
flowing 40–60 word paragraph (or the supplied narration_policy word range), not
separate spoken lines. Address the viewer as "you". Sound like someone calmly
describing a bizarre place they know well. This is neither a survival checklist
nor poetic horror. The world supplies the strangeness; ordinary sentences can be calm.

Treat approved_facts as background material, not headings to cover. Choose just
3–4 interesting facts for each candidate. Include 1–2 concrete sensory/environmental
details and at least one ordinary activity you would spend time doing there.
Sensory detail can establish what living here feels like without teaching a survival
lesson. Let the visuals communicate everything else. No category-completion quota.
Never mechanically cover food, water, shelter, inhabitants, danger and escape.

Use at most ONE main danger/rule, optionally its consequence. A warning, protective
action and consequence belonging to the same mechanism count as one main danger;
do not slip in a second threat or unrelated rule. Zero dangers is allowed if the
world and daily life already make an interesting travelogue. No required ominous ending.

Write natural connected prose. Link ideas with ordinary transitions such as "and",
"but", "while", "when", "so" and "after" when they fit. Vary sentence structure.
Do not join a list of commands with "and" and call it a travelogue. Do not force a
transition into every sentence. Avoid ornate language, AI clichés, standalone creepy
observations, clever reveal lines, rhetorical endings and obligatory twists.
Most sentences should describe living here, not warn the viewer about everything.

Invent NO facts or mechanics. Ground sensory details and ordinary activities in the
approved facts too. No invented colours, sounds, supplies, safe areas, schedules or
causal links. Keep relevant qualifications when using a fact, but omit whole facts
rather than cramming the entire system into 60 words. The title supplies no extra lore.

For each candidate return its paragraph and review metadata:
- facts: 3–4 distinct facts used, each with source (an approved_facts key) and quote
  (a verbatim supporting excerpt from that fact). Do not bundle a checklist into a quote.
- sensory_details: 1–2 verbatim excerpts FROM YOUR PARAGRAPH containing those details.
- ordinary_activity: a verbatim excerpt FROM YOUR PARAGRAPH describing the activity.
- main_danger_or_rule: the one mechanism used, or null. This is metadata, not extra prose.
Generate alternative perspectives on this SAME spec, never alternative worlds.
If previous_attempt exists, address its review issues without changing approved_facts.
