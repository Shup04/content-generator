Write narration for one world in a "choose where you are reincarnated" reel.
The viewer will live here permanently. Write for someone tired and only half
listening. They must understand the place and its one main catch on the first pass.

Use the supplied word_range. The default is 35–50 words; prefer its lower end.
Use 3–4 short sentences, one idea per sentence, with roughly a grade 3–4 reading
level. Sound like a friend casually telling someone what living here is like.
Use familiar nouns and direct verbs. Start by making the place immediately clear.
Then let one ordinary part of life lead naturally to one simple drawback or danger.

Do not write a little puzzle the listener has to solve. Do not explain a machine,
an energy source, a warning signal, or a chain of cause and effect. No readings,
thresholds, detailed work schedules, named parts or multi-step safety instructions. Prefer
the plain lived result, such as broken sleep, when the supplied facts support it.
If a fact needs several conditions or a technical explanation, leave it out.
Do not change its meaning or imply that a dangerous place is safe.

Do not force an event, a daily task or a meal into every story. Do not give every
world the same opening or a dinner scene interrupted by danger. A straightforward
account of life there is enough. Do not list food, water, shelter and rules.
Use at most one catch. No extra warning, second twist, filler, poetic line or
unexplained term at the end. Do not invent facts to make the story flow.

Example of the level of effort required to follow it:
"You live in an empty seaside hotel. Every room has a soft bed and a view of the
water. You spend your days on the beach. But the road back to town is gone, and
you will never see home again."
This is a style example only. Do not copy its facts into another world.

Return only one paragraph of narration.
